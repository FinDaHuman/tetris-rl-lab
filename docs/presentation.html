01

The game

What ALE/Tetris-v5 actually is, and the mechanics that decide everything downstream.

Part 1 · the environment

It is an Atari 2600 ROM,
running under emulation.

Not a Tetris library. A 1980s cartridge, emulated by Stella, wrapped by the Arcade Learning Environment. You cannot read its memory or its rules.

You are given

  • a screen — 210×160×3 RGB
  • an action — one of five
  • a reward — one number

You are not given

  • the board as an array
  • the piece identity or position
  • the next piece
  • any rule of the game

Part 1 · observation

This is the game.
This is what the agent sees.

The Atari screen — 210×160×3 RGB
What Track 1's network is fed — 84×84 grayscale, ×4 stacked
Why stack 4 frames? One still image cannot tell you which way a piece is moving. Stacking the last four gives the network motion. Standard since the 2015 DQN Atari paper.

Part 1 · action space

Five actions. No hard drop.

>>> env.unwrapped.get_action_meanings()
['NOOP', 'FIRE', 'RIGHT', 'LEFT', 'DOWN']        # Discrete(5)
  • FIRE = rotate. One direction only — there is no counter-clockwise.
  • DOWN = soft drop. It nudges the piece down one step.
  • There is no hard-drop action. To get a piece down you wait for gravity, or you hold DOWN.
My own custom engine does have HARD_DROP — one action, piece slams down, done. The Atari ROM simply does not.

Part 1 · consequence

So my planner presses DOWN
ninety times.

agents/ale/ale_tetris_agent.py:546
actions = [FIRE] * fires + [move] * moves + [DOWN] * 90

Rotate n times, shift sideways n times, then spam the soft-drop ninety times to seat the piece.

It looks absurd until you know there is no drop action. Then it is the only thing you can write.

Part 1 · reward

The reward is lines cleared.
Nothing else.

ALE Tetris pays you for

  • clearing a line

and nothing else

  • no points for surviving
  • no points for dropping
  • no points for placing a piece

So when my agent scores 37.0 reward, that literally means 37 lines.

Say "37 lines." The "3,700" you may see in my code is my own unit (lines × 100) for a friendlier headline — it is not the ROM's score.

Part 1 · the consequence that defines this project

This is why pure RL scores exactly zero.

A random policy must place
~10 pieces correctly in a row
to clear one line
→
it essentially never
does this by chance
→
reward is 0 on every
transition, for all 10M steps
so the advantage estimate is 0
→
the policy gradient is 0

PPO did not fail to learn. It had nothing to learn from. You cannot fix a missing signal by collecting more samples of it.

Verified live: NOOP-only from seed 0 tops out after 416 agent steps with total reward 0.0.

Part 1 · three things that trip people up

The fine print.

Frame-skip 4

One action runs for 4 emulator frames. So 10M agent steps = 40M frames — about 20% of the canonical 200M-frame Atari budget, not the 2% a naive reading suggests. State the unit.

Sticky actions 0.25

25% chance the emulator repeats your previous action instead of the new one. It stops agents memorising one fixed button sequence.

⚠ The seed does nothing

The ALE seed does not change Tetris's piece sequence. Seeds 0/1/2/42 give a bit-identical trajectory. So sticky actions are the only randomness in this environment — and "10 seeds" is one game, ten times.

Part 2 · motivation

Why five tracks, honestly.

  • Pure RL on ALE Tetris scores zero — and I needed to know whether that was my bug or the problem's nature. The only way to tell is to change one thing at a time. Keep the method, change the environment (Track 3) → it starts clearing lines. Keep the environment, change the method (Track 2) → it scores. That isolates the cause.
  • I did not know what was allowed. "Make an RL agent" could mean strictly model-free learning, or it could permit search, planning and hand-authored features — a lot of published "RL Tetris" does exactly that. Rather than guess and risk submitting against the wrong interpretation, I built both, and drew an enforced boundary between them (AGENTS.md + tests).
  • It turns a zero into a finding. "PPO got 0" is a shrug. "PPO got 0 on pixels, 1.04 lines on a structured observation, and a planner on the same engine and the same laptop got ~190× more" is a result.
Be honest about the ordering: this began as a hedge against an ambiguous brief, and became a controlled comparison. It was not a grand design from day one.

Same seed. Same algorithm.
Different action space.

seed 1002: 2 lines, 31 pieces

The comparison is fair on experience, not just step count. A Track 3 step is one keypress; a Track 5 step is a whole piece. Track 3 spends a measured 9.11 keypresses per piece, so its 100M steps ≈ 11.0M pieces against Track 5's 12.0M — matched within 9%, in a quarter of the wall-clock.

Part 2 · what is still different

Track 5 → Track 4: three more changes.

1. Hand-authored features

Track 4 is handed holes, heights, bumpiness, wells, transitions, landing height. Track 5 must learn all of it from raw cells.

2. Lookahead

Track 4 clones the engine and searches 2 pieces deep over the real queue. Track 5 sees the same one-piece preview and cannot search at all.

3. Optimizer

Track 4 fits 10 weights with CEM. Track 5 fits ~150k network weights with PPO from a scalar reward.

Track 5 closes the action-space part of the gap. These three are what remain — and Part 3 shows they are worth 97.7% of it.

Part 2 · the question I will be asked

"Are all five reinforcement learning?"

No — and I will concede that before you ask.

RL  Tracks 1, 3, 5

PPO. A policy network and a value network. Learns from the reward at every step via the policy gradient and an advantage estimate. This is reinforcement learning.

not RL  Tracks 2, 4

CEM. No value function, no TD error, no policy gradient. It collapses an entire episode into one scalar and never asks which move caused what. It is derivative-free policy search — black-box optimisation.

Sutton & Barto, §1.1 exclude evolutionary methods for exactly this reason: "they do not notice which states an individual passes through during its lifetime, or which actions it selects." — Structurally, Track 4 is a small chess engine: hand-crafted eval + shallow search + tuned coefficients.

Part 3 · the finding

I was wrong,
and I ran the experiment that proved it.

My report originally claimed the action abstraction was the dominant variable — reasoning from the literature, where essentially every strong RL-Tetris result uses placement actions.

Track 5 tested that claim on my own engine and refuted it. The abstraction is real — 5.4×, at matched experience, in a quarter of the wall-clock — but it is necessary, not sufficient. It closes 2.3% of the gap. The hand-authored features and the search carry the rest.

The caveat I have to state myself: Track 5's learning curve had not converged — ep_rew_mean was still climbing 60 → 75 over the final 4M steps. So 5.60 is a lower bound, not a ceiling. I have shown the action space is not sufficient at this compute budget. I have not shown hand-authored features are necessary in principle. (Track 3, by contrast, plateaued at 36M of its 100M steps.)

Part 3 · receipts

Four things I will not let you
catch me on.

  • "Score 3,700" is my own unit, not the ROM's. ALE Tetris's native reward is lines cleared — my agent gets 37.0 reward. If you ask what I scored, the answer is 37 lines.
  • My "10 seeds" for Track 2 are one game, ten times. The ALE seed does not change the piece sequence — I verified it. The zero variance is an artifact, not robustness. Effective N = 1. (What is genuine: it still clears 37 with sticky actions at 0.25, because the planner re-reads the board every piece.)
  • Track 4's "200-line ceiling" is my piece cap, not the agent. 500 pieces × 4 cells ÷ 10 columns = 200. Uncapped it does not top out — 10,000 pieces, 3,997 lines, still alive. Read its score as ≈ 0.4 × cap.
  • Track 5 had not converged, so 5.60 is a floor, not a ceiling.
Scores are not comparable across tracks — Track 3's includes drop points, Track 4's does not, Tracks 1–2 use my lines×100 convention. Compare lines, within an environment.

Part 3 · what I would do next

Two experiments, in order.

1. Run Track 5 to convergence

It was still improving when its 12M-step budget ran out. 50–100M steps (~8–16 h at the measured 1,664 steps/s) would find the actual ceiling of the action abstraction on its own. Right now I only have a floor.

2. A features-only ablation

Track 5 isolated the action space. Nothing isolated the features. Feed the 10 Dellacherie features to Track 5's PPO — still no search — and the remaining 97.7% splits into "better state representation" vs "lookahead + CEM". That would fully decompose the 190×.
